## Decisions taken

**D-O3 — with fuel loss, `delta` becomes the traded volume.** Gas stored and gas traded part
company once a fraction is burnt: one clip into inventory takes `1/(1 − loss)` clips out of
the market. `exp_ex` stays physical and still nets to zero; `delta` carries the multiplier on
the injection leg only. Measured on a deterministic tree, where `E[S | exercise] = F` makes it
exact: 1.015228 injecting and 1.000000 withdrawing at 1.5 % loss.

The choice was not a preference. It is the convention under which
`sum_i DF_i · delta_i · F_i == V0` still closes, at **1.8e-15** for every loss tested. Had
`delta` stayed the inventory volume the identity would have needed a separate fuel term and
the reported hedge would no longer reprice the deal — which is how we know it is right rather
than merely plausible.

**The inventory bound constrains the balance the day OPENS with.** Reported on the closing
balance the same schedule reads 41.00 clips against 42.00 — a clip short. Both are fair
readings of "1 April inventory" and a contract must say which. Documented rather than chosen;
that choice is still P1.1's.

**P2.2, the `1e-6` tie threshold: considered and declined.** It is absolute on values that
scale with the deal, so wrong in shape. Measured, it does not bite at realistic rates — the
same deal across seven orders of magnitude at 10 % funding gives a per-MWh value identical to
eight decimals. It *is* reachable at r ≈ 1e-9, where a 600 MWh deal cycles once and a 60,000
MWh deal twice. It cannot misprice a contract (nominal cash identical to nine decimals either
way, because the extra turn is worth nothing) but it doubles the reported hedge. Left as is;
the conditions that would justify revisiting are recorded on the roadmap.

**Calibration and backtesting deferred.** The data for both is already in `ttf q.xlsx` —
4,117 day-ahead prints beside the forward curves and all fourteen mid-year dates from 2011 to
2024 with a full realised year after them. It is a harness to write, not data to find.
~~no gap over three days~~ **corrected**: three days is the spacing of the date index. Between
numeric DA observations the longest gap is **five calendar days**, with 34 intervals over
three, and some far-forward columns have hundreds of missing entries. 55 columns are not a
complete 55-contract panel.

## Behaviour established

- ~~**Ratchets cap a store through the exit, not the entrance.**~~ **WITHDRAWN — see the
  correction notice.** The exit does constrain a store, but the number below is a property
  of the 240-clip grid. As written: under a realistic profile this store peaks at **52.5 %**
  full — injection still runs at 8 clips/day below half, but
  withdrawal ratchets to 0.30 near empty, so it can only fill to what it can still empty
  before the window closes. The cliff is sharp: 0.35 fails, 0.40 works, because at 4 clips/day
  a multiplier under 0.375 floors to 1 clip/day and the last stretch alone takes two months.
- ~~**Ratchets and dated bounds can be jointly infeasible.**~~ **WITHDRAWN.** They can be, but
  not here: the 70 % floor is met on a 480-clip grid with the same inputs. As written: each
  is fine alone; together the 70 % floor became unreachable and the model refused it.
  Pushing with floors on six consecutive days still only reached 59.2 %, so it was physical,
  not economic. The six extra floors were on the same coarse grid and could not have
  distinguished the two explanations.
- ~~**Refining the clip is free and answer-neutral.**~~ **WITHDRAWN for ratcheted deals.** The
  unratcheted value is identical at 60 and 240 states, and that is the *only* case in which
  it holds: the rates are exactly expressible there, so the check could not fail. Ratcheted,
  240 → 3,840 clips moves the value **+70.59 %**.
- **Intrinsic delta is the intrinsic schedule.** With one price state `E[S | exercise] = F`,
  so the hedge and the schedule coincide to 3.6e-12. For a store **with no fuel loss** it nets
  to zero, which means every MWh of net hedge is extrinsic. With fuel the physical cycle still
  closes but the market one does not: at 1.5 % the net hedge is −1.522843 MWh per 100 MWh
  cycled, exactly the gas burnt. Corrected 2026-09-10 (evening).
- **A flat-per-month curve makes some churn exactly free.** Injecting and withdrawing inside
  one month is precisely break-even, so the optimiser is indifferent and any non-zero rate
  tips it into doing it: volume 600,000 → 1,200,000 MWh while nominal cash is unchanged at
  6,320,000. Measure a store by its cash, not by how much gas it moves.
- **The hedge is trustworthy in some months and not others.** Oct–Dec swing 0.1 % on a 1 %
  move; Apr–Aug swing about half the book's largest position, because six months of the test
  curve are priced within a rounding error of each other and the optimiser flips between
  months it cannot tell apart.

## What this leaves open

**P4.1 — the rationale below was withdrawn the same evening; see the correction notice.** A
common long factor adds *exactly nothing* to a zero-fee store, verified to 1e-14, so the
storage case for a two-factor lattice is gone. The correlations stand, the magnitudes do not.
As written:

**P4.1, a second factor, is now the largest item and the evidence for it is no longer
theoretical.** A one-factor model forces every pair of forwards to correlate exactly 1.000.
The market does not: on daily log returns since 2015, c1 against c6 realises **0.801**, c6
against c12 **0.771**, c1 against c24 **0.633**. The c6/c12 spread realises **0.373** of
annualised vol where the model gives it about **0.041** — roughly **nine times less**. That
is why storage extrinsic comes out at 4.2 % of value and *rises* with mean reversion: it is
measuring short-term cycling, not the seasonal spread a store exists to monetise. Detailed
plan in [DESIGN-P4.1-two-factor.md](DESIGN-P4.1-two-factor.md).

**Still verified, not validated.** Everything today widened what the model can represent and
made it fail louder where it cannot. None of it establishes that the numbers are right for a
traded price. That gate is calibration plus the backtest, both deliberately parked.

The remaining P1 items are unchanged: tunnel semantics (which balance), curve-shape acceptance
criteria, and the integer-clip representation — for which the ratchet work is a stronger
argument than the rate work was.
